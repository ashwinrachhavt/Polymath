What happens, and how long it takes

An ordinary shard is done in about three quarters of a second. The one that completes a stone takes about a second and a third. The limit in the brief is two.

Why it should still feel good the fiftieth time

It never takes the screen. The reward happens beside the quiz, in the place where the stone will stay, so there is nothing to dismiss. The Next button works the whole time.

It is short and it ends the same way every time. The pleasure is the shard landing and the piece filling in, which tells you something true: this idea is one step closer. The arc bends a different way for each idea so repeated runs are not identical.

The big moment is rare by design. Three shards make a stone, so the longer sequence plays once per idea, not once per answer.

With reduced motion on, nothing flies or scales. The piece fades in where it belongs and the name appears. Sound is off unless the learner turns it on.

The stone forms at the Fresh-cut finish, which is matte. The gloss is saved for the first review, so polishing later has something to give.
