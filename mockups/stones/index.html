Stones you can read

A proposal for Polymath's reward layer. Every idea a learner proves becomes one stone. Its shape says what kind of idea it is, its material says the subject, and its finish says how well it has held. Four boards follow; this page says what I would choose and what needs your decision.

1. Rendering directions

The same six stones drawn three ways, with what each costs. Recommendation: vector art everywhere, live light for one stone at a time.

2. The inventory

Six shapes, twelve materials, four finishes, 288 named stones, a geode for a finished mission, and how an idea gets its stone.

3. The earning moment

A quiz you can play. A shard flies to its idea; the third one forms the stone and names it. 0.76 s for a shard, 1.34 s for a stone.

4. The wall

Stones grouped by mission, in four states, with an empty state and a share image for a finished mission.

Decisions that are yours

  1. How broad a subject is. With twelve broad subjects, a specialist's wall leans on a few materials: the Rust mission on board 4 is mostly Obsidian. Keep it (the wall honestly shows where you spend your time), or split the subjects your learners use most into more materials.
  2. The six kinds of idea. Fact, principle, mechanism, pattern, tool, method. These need to match what the concept extractor can label reliably. A seventh, such as an example or case, would need a seventh shape.
  3. Shards per stone. The boards use three. Fewer makes stones cheap; more makes the first one slow to arrive.
  4. What earns each finish. The boards use one review, three reviews and six months. These are placeholders for your review schedule.
  5. Material names. Real mineral names read well and are easy to say. Check that none clash with names you already use: today's seven stones include Topaz, Ruby and Sapphire, which this set does not reuse.
  6. Photographic stones. I could not test image-model plates because the Gemini key has no image quota. If you want to see that direction properly, it needs a paid image tier.
