# Market research: Amie

**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Analyst:** overnight build session · **Status:** current as of 2026-10-25 sources
**Category:** AI meeting notes + personal OS (pivoted from calendar).

## What it is

Formerly the "joyful productivity" calendar/todos app, Amie has pivoted to
**AI meeting intelligence**: botless call recording from the Mac notch,
context-aware summaries with action items, post-meeting automation, and -
notably - an **MCP server** exposing meetings, calendar, email, and todos to
Claude/ChatGPT. The third confirmed pivot in this research set (with Tana
and Mem) from "organized notes/calendar" to "agents that do the work." [S1]

## Current feature set (verified 2026-10-25)

- **Botless recording**: Mac-notch (or floating) UI records any call without
  a bot joining; pause for off-record, auto-stop on silence, split by room,
  speaker separation with remembered names. [S1]
- **Context-aware summaries + action items**: claims business-context-aware
  summaries where generic tools "miss the mark"; 17 languages with speaker
  labeling, 82 more without. [S1]
- **Post-meeting automation ("within 47 seconds")**: share summary, update
  CRM, plan action items, schedule the next meeting. [S1]
- **Auto todo suggestions** from calls, addable in one click. [S1]
- **MCP server**: grants Claude/ChatGPT access to meeting notes, calendar,
  emails, and todos. [S1]
- Email and calendar remain in the product. [S1]

## Business model

Freemium with paid tiers (historically ~$10-25/mo; not re-verified tonight).
Positions against Fireflies, Otter, Fathom. [S1]

## Strengths

- **Botless capture is a real UX wedge**: no awkward "OtterPilot has joined"
  - and it works for in-person meetings too.
- The 47-second post-meeting pipeline is the clearest articulation anywhere
  of meetings-to-action.
- Shipping an MCP server is a forward-leaning bet: make the personal OS
  queryable by whatever assistant the user already pays for.

## Weaknesses

- Meeting-centric: knowledge outside conversations (reading, ideas, tasks
  from other surfaces) is thin.
- Summaries are still unverified generation; "understands your business" is
  a claim without provenance.
- The pivot strands the original calendar/todos identity; feature depth
  outside meetings is unclear.

## What Axiom can assimilate

1. **The post-meeting pipeline**: transcript -> verified summary -> action
   items as proposals -> follow-up scheduling. Axiom's meeting artifacts
   (#286) plus the proposal pattern can express this entire loop with
   receipts. The "47 seconds" framing is the bar: fast, automatic,
   reviewable.
2. **Action items as one-click proposals** from meeting notes - same
   pattern as capture proposals, reusing the proposals seam.
3. **Botless recording UX** (pause/off-record, auto-stop) if/when Axiom
   records audio - the trust ergonomics matter more than the transcription.
4. **MCP as a distribution surface** (decision item, do NOT build
   unprompted): exposing Axiom's verified graph over MCP would let users'
   existing assistants query their knowledge. Worth a conversation about
   scope, authz, and the read/write boundary.

## Where Axiom can differentiate

1. **Meetings enter the same verified graph** as reading, capture, and
   tasks - Amie's meetings are an island.
2. **Verified summaries**: claims proven against the transcript before
   saving (the zettel pipeline), where Amie asks for trust.
3. **Cross-time memory**: a meeting connects to what the user read, decided,
   and did - not just to other meetings.
