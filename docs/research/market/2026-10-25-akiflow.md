# Market research: Akiflow

**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Analyst:** overnight build session · **Status:** current as of 2026-10-25 sources
**Category:** Calendar-first task consolidation + AI assistant ("Aki").

## What it is

Y Combinator-backed "one app for tasks & calendars powered by AI": a
calendar-first command center that consolidates tasks from everywhere into a
universal inbox, time-blocks them automatically, and adds an always-on chat
assistant ("Aki") that schedules, moves, and plans on command. Targets
"founders, operators, and obsessed doers." [S1]

## Current feature set (verified 2026-10-25)

- **One synced calendar** across work and personal accounts. [S1]
- **Universal Inbox**: tasks captured from 10+ native integrations
  (incl. email, Slack, Notion, Asana, etc.) into one triage stream. [S1]
- **Automatic time-blocking**: tasks placed on the calendar "in one click";
  smart time slots that work around existing events; AI nudges to stay
  goal-aligned. [S1]
- **Aki assistant**: natural-language scheduling ("You have 1h slot at 8 AM.
  Shall I block it?"), moves events, answers agenda questions, plans from
  chat. [S1]
- **Planning horizons**: someday / this month / this week triage views. [S1]
- Team scheduling; Apple Watch / iCloud Calendar support. [S1]

## Business model

Paid subscription with 7-day trial (historically ~$19-34/mo; not re-verified
tonight). YC-backed. [S1]

## Strengths

- **Consolidation executed well**: the universal inbox is the feature users
  cite - every task source into one triage, then onto the calendar.
- Aki's chat-ops over your own calendar (move, block, check) is the
  assistant interaction done at the right altitude.
- Speed/keyboard-first reputation; strong with operators.

## Weaknesses

- **Action without knowledge**: nothing is learned from the tasks; no
  memory, links, or accumulation. The assistant is a dispatcher, not a
  second brain.
- Opaque auto-scheduling: when the plan is wrong, correcting it is manual.
- Price friction and a crowded calendar-assistant field (Motion, Reclaim,
  Vimcal).

## What Axiom can assimilate

1. **Chat-ops over the daybook**: "block an hour for X," "move check-in to
   tomorrow," "do I have time for Y tomorrow" - a small set of
   calendar/task verbs for the assistant, exactly the altitude Aki proves
   works. The daybook + tasks domains already hold the state these verbs
   need.
2. **Universal-inbox triage** maps one-to-one onto capture triage
   (ASH-272): Akiflow's inbox is the same intake shape, validating the
   triage UX.
3. **Planning horizons (someday/month/week)** as task list facets - cheap
   additive filters on the existing tasks domain.

## Where Axiom can differentiate

1. **The assistant remembers**: Aki dispatches; Alfred answers with evidence
   from the user's verified knowledge, and its actions write back into a
   compounding store.
2. **Proposals with provenance** vs. opaque auto-scheduling.
3. **One substrate**: tasks, calendar, knowledge, and capture share one
   graph; Akiflow is tasks+calendar only.
