# Market research: Sunsama

**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Analyst:** overnight build session · **Status:** current as of 2026-10-25 sources
**Category:** Guided daily planning (calm productivity).

## What it is

The reference implementation of the **daily planning ritual**: task manager +
calendar + daily planner built around a deliberate morning plan and an
evening shutdown, positioned against burnout rather than toward throughput.
"Start calm. Stay focused. End confident." [S1]

## Current feature set (verified 2026-10-25)

- **Guided daily planning**: morning ritual aligning goals, prioritizing
  tasks, setting a realistic workload. [S1]
- **Timeboxing**: drag planned tasks onto a unified multi-calendar view. [S1]
- **Universal task intake**: pull tasks from Asana, Trello, Jira, Linear,
  Gmail, Outlook, Slack, Teams, Todoist, GitHub, ClickUp, Monday, Notion. [S1]
- **Focus mode** with per-task actual-vs-planned time tracking, distraction
  muting (Slack/Teams status sync), break reminders. [S1]
- **Daily highlights**: automatic end-of-day summary of what shipped. [S1]
- **Time analytics**: where the week actually went, by category. [S1]
- **Daily shutdown ritual**: a 5pm "wrap up your day" prompt to close work
  "without guilt." [S1]
- **Weekly objectives** and weekly review. [S1]

## Business model

Paid subscription (~$20/mo historically; not re-verified tonight), premium
priced against free task apps on the strength of the ritual. [S1]

## Strengths

- **The ritual is the product**: planning and shutdown ceremonies create the
  behavior change users credit Sunsama with - structure, not features.
- Planned-vs-actual time per task closes the estimation loop almost nobody
  else attempts.
- Calm, anti-hustle positioning is a durable emotional moat.

## Weaknesses

- **Manual by conviction**: planning is the user's job; AI assistance is
  minimal. For users drowning in intake, the ritual itself becomes a task.
- No knowledge layer - tasks are tasks; nothing is learned, linked, or
  remembered.
- Premium price for a planner is a hard sell against Motion/Reclaim-style
  automation.

## What Axiom can assimilate

1. **The shutdown ritual** - directly informs ASH-305. Sunsama's 5pm wrap
   (unfinished tasks rolled, day closed cleanly, "without guilt") is the
   validated shape: a guided close, not a notification.
2. **Planned vs. actual time** per task as daybook data: cheap to record,
   and it powers the analytics that make the weekly review real.
3. **Morning planning as a guided flow** (align goals -> pick tasks -> check
   realistic load) for the daybook surface.
4. **Unified intake from many tools**: Axiom's integrations already pull
   Gmail/Calendar/Linear; Sunsama proves users value the pull-through-daily
   model over sync-everything.

## Where Axiom can differentiate

1. **The plan writes itself**: Sunsama plans manually; Axiom's assistant can
   draft the day from tasks, calendar, and capture triage - the ritual kept,
   the labor removed.
2. **Knowledge accrues**: Sunsama forgets; Axiom's day becomes daybook
   artifacts, verified summaries, and open questions that compound.
3. **Proposals, not just planning**: unplanned items arrive as triaged
   proposals with provenance.
