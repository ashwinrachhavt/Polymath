# Market research: Anytype

**Date:** 2026-10-25 · **Analyst:** overnight build session · **Status:** current as of 2026-10-25 sources
**Category:** Local-first, encrypted, P2P object-based workspace.

## What it is

The hardest-line privacy architecture in the category: local-first object
store, on-device encryption where only the user holds keys, offline account
creation, peer-to-peer sync with no gatekeeper, open protocols. Positioned
as "a safe haven for digital collaboration" - private chats, documents, and
databases in one. [S1]

## Current feature set (verified 2026-10-25)

- **Local-first vault**: on-device encryption, user-held keys, offline
  account creation. [S1]
- **P2P sync** on local networks; self-hostable backups; no server
  gatekeeper. [S1]
- **Object model**: everything (person, task, document) is a typed object
  with relations; graph and database views. [S1]
- **Block editor, databases, templates, widgets.** [S1]
- **Private chats** alongside documents (collaboration without a cloud
  operator). [S1]

## Business model

Freemium storage/network tiers (not re-verified tonight); open-source
clients, self-hosting encouraged. [S1]

## Strengths

- The strongest credible privacy story in the market - architectural, not
  promised.
- Typed objects + relations: a real data model under the notes, closer to
  Axiom's domains than to flat files.
- Collaboration without trusting a server is a genuine technical
  achievement.

## Weaknesses

- **No AI layer**: privacy maximalism currently excludes hosted intelligence;
  the assistant direction is unsolved for them.
- Complexity: objects/types/relations are powerful but demand modeling from
  the user.
- Sync and collaboration UX carry the friction P2P always carries.

## What Axiom can assimilate

1. **Typed objects with relations as the mental model** - Axiom's domains
   already are this; Anytype proves users can learn it when the UI teaches
   it (graph + database views over the same data).
2. **The privacy story as architecture**: Axiom's registry gates, quota
   ledger, and `sensitiveDataAllowed: false` provider flags are the seed of
   a comparable "trust by construction" narrative - worth writing down as
   such (docs already exist; the marketing page doesn't).
3. **Templates for object creation** - small additive UX for note/task
   creation.

## Where Axiom can differentiate

1. **Intelligence with gates**: Anytype can't easily offer hosted AI;
   Axiom's free-first provider registry + explicit paid-usage decisions is
   the middle path - AI that is off until the owner turns it on, with
   budgets in code.
2. **Cloud-native collaboration and integrations** (Gmail, Calendar, Drive,
   Linear) that a P2P architecture can't match.
3. **Action and verification**: proposals, extraction, obligations - the
   work layer Anytype doesn't have.
